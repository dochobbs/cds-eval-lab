What this lab measures

Three things matter at the point of care, and they have to be measured separately.

Clinical AI benchmarks tend to collapse into one number, which obscures the failure modes a clinician actually cares about. The harness here keeps three dimensions apart so a system can't trade one for another and look fine on average.

The first is content quality — accuracy, completeness, and specificity scored on real ambulatory queries. The second is hallucination safety, split into two distinct probes: known clinical traps (a beta-lactam allergy paired with a request to dose a beta-lactam) and fabricated entities (an invented trial name in a plausible-sounding query). The third is freshness, which asks whether the system knows what changed in the guidelines this year.

A system that scores 90 on content but caves to false premises is not safer than one that scores 75 and refuses to be wrong. The leaderboard further down makes that visible.
